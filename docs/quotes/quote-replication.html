GAP 1

Quotes are priced per cohort, not per specimen

Blocks 4 of 5 quotes · needs your decision before I build it

This is the big one. §11 prices a full quote per specimen type — one price for FFPE, another for plasma. Not one of these five quotes does that. Four price per cohort:

$1,650 / subject (Cohort #1)
$1,450 / subject (Cohort #2)
$825 / subject (non-cancerous bladder) Pan-cancer and benign diseases

The per-cohort table already exists — but only in the extension track. The full quote has never had it. That is backwards: extensions are the simpler case, and the full quote is where cohorts actually differ in price.

Decision I would make per-cohort the default for a full quote and keep per-specimen as the alternative, since four of five real quotes need the former and none need the latter. Confirm and I will rebuild §11 that way.
GAP 2

GAP 3

Cost lines that are neither specimen nor shipping

Blocks 3 of 5 quotes · I can build this now

Three quotes carry a priced line the form has no home for:

  • $100 / subject — Medrio entry, which alone is $53,200 of a $948,425 quote
  • $50 / subject stool — shipping kits to sites, the opposite direction from §10
  • $150 / translated colonoscopy report — reports are a section in the form but carry no price

§11 has "other costs", but they are flat amounts. Each of these is a rate times a count. Making other-cost lines optionally per-unit covers all three, and the reports section can then price itself.

GAP 9

Commitments and conditions in prose

Appears in all 5 quotes · discussion

Each quote carries commitments that sit in no section:

  • "Natera will not terminate this study early if MT Group is enrolling subjects as expected."
  • "Natera may halt the study with 4 weeks' notice."
  • "If freight charges increase dramatically during the study execution, shipping & handling costs may also be increased."
  • "MT Group will invoice based upon the actual samples delivered."
  • "It is acceptable that subjects enrolled in this study also enroll in another Natera study with MT Group."

§12's notes box can hold them, but they would arrive as one undifferentiated block. They are not Terms and Conditions — those are boilerplate and identical across all five. These are deal-specific, and they are the terms most likely to be argued about later.

Decision A short list of named clauses a rep ticks and edits — early termination, halt notice, freight escalation, invoice basis, cross-enrolment — would make these consistent instead of remembered. Worth it, or is free text fine?

What already fits, and is worth knowing

  • The butterfly quote replicates exactly. Four Streck tubes, two drawn with a straight needle from one arm and two with a butterfly from the other — the per-tube blocks hold the type, size and handling of each one separately.
  • Subcohorts hold the pan-cancer tables. Eleven cancer subcohorts and six benign ones, each with its own count, summing to the cohort — exactly the shape already built.
  • Geography lives in the inclusions — "Collected in Turkey", "Collected in the United States" — which is the call we made against the feasibility form's geography field, now confirmed by four real quotes.
  • "No additional inclusions or exclusions" appears per cohort, not once at the end. I had recorded it as a closing line; the adenoma quote prints it after each cohort's criteria. I have corrected the spec.
  • The US-destination sentence is real — "batch shipped on dry ice to a United States location designated by Natera" — almost exactly the wording built last round.

One thing I would not copy

The five quotes disagree with themselves on headings and on the letterhead. "Summary" in three, "Study Objectives" in one; "Cohorts" in two, "Size" and "Populations and Size" elsewhere. The address reads Van Nuys, CA 91406 in four and Los Angeles, CA 91406 in the fifth — same street, two cities.

The builder should settle on one set of headings and one address rather than reproduce the drift. I need you to tell me which address is right.
